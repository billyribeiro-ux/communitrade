# Maintenance durability and later roadmap

A 10–20 year business requires maintenance and customer retention; no current framework/version provides a longevity guarantee. Preserve product/data contracts and ownership while keeping implementations supported.

## Operating cadence

- Each release: relevant regressions, migration/restore consequences, supplier capability changes, notices and changelog.
- Weekly during beta: errors, support/moderation demand, data freshness/cost, activation/return, unresolved trade follow-up and failed jobs.
- Monthly: dependencies/security patches, provider usage/spend, backup/alert delivery and account access review.
- Quarterly: restore drill, export portability, abuse/privacy review, capacity evidence, paid conversion/churn and commercial data economics.
- At least annually: supported Node/framework/database lifecycle plan, provider portability/contract review, data retention and legal policy review, actual business model and staffing.

Urgent security/corruption patches use a focused reviewed change; the schedule does not postpone them. Upgrade LTS/database majors before end of support; do not keep 2026 pins indefinitely. Retain reproducible deployment artifacts and calculation-version fixtures when upgrading.

## Portability

Keep stable user/instrument/publication/event IDs, provider adapters, standard OIDC, PostgreSQL schema/migrations and open API contracts. Test exports without a hosting-specific database API. Store licensed provenance and erasure/retention obligations with content. Avoid coupling core positions/journal records to a single chart/rendering/provider object model.

## Phase 2 and beyond

Phase 2: livestreaming, screen sharing, host audio, rooms, moderation and persistent live room chat; separate future requirements/cost/provider/release decision. No hidden streaming dependency is added to Phase 1.

Later: stock/options/futures scanners, institutional holdings/flow criteria, real-time data entitlements, broker-connected verification, expanded instruments/strategies, creator monetization, native apps and enterprise features. Each requires its own data/commercial/verification evidence.

The original ambition to promote high-win-rate members is reserved for a separately specified trustworthy discovery/ranking system. Do not release a manipulated/unverified leaderboard as a shortcut. Phase 1's useful publishing/discussion/journal workflow must earn retention first.

## Business evidence

Track the MVP's activation/return/contributor/review/report/conversion/churn hypotheses against actual cohorts. Pricing and cost models are experiments, not income forecasts. Fund future scope from measured evidence and adequate operating capacity rather than assuming new features repair poor retention.
